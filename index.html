Machine Setup — Pimp Q&A
⚡
What pressure indicates the O₂ cylinder is full?
›
2000 PSI = full. Below 500 PSI → change cylinder. Wall supply should read ≥50 PSI. Always check both pipeline and cylinder sources at the start of every case.
⚡
What is the significance of a failed circuit leak test?
›
Indicates a leak in the breathing system — patient may receive sub-therapeutic gas concentrations and desaturate. Find the leak source (connections, ETT, vaporizer filler cap, CO₂ absorber) before proceeding. Never bypass this test.
⚡
Why check CO₂ absorber color?
›
Purple = exhausted (DO NOT USE). White/clear = fresh. Spent absorber allows CO₂ rebreathing → hypercarbia → respiratory acidosis → cardiovascular compromise. Change before the case if any doubt.
⚡
What is the APL valve and when do you use manual vs auto?
›
Adjustable Pressure Limiting valve — controls max circuit pressure. Manual mode: bag ventilation (set to 20–30 cmH₂O). Auto/vent mode: ventilator takes over. Failure to switch modes = inability to ventilate or uncontrolled pressure. This is a classic pimp question.
⚡
What is the minimum safe O₂ flow rate?
›
200 mL/min O₂ is the minimum to prevent hypoxic gas delivery. Most machines have a safety interlock preventing pure N₂O delivery. The Dräger Fabius GS has a proportioning system that maintains minimum 25% O₂.
⚡
Walk me through the order of monitors you apply and why.
›
SpO₂ first (baseline oxygenation) → EKG (cardiac rhythm, baseline HR) → BP (baseline hemodynamics) → ETCO₂ after intubation (confirm placement, ventilation). Temperature as indicated. The order follows the priority of what kills you fastest if missed.

Pre-op Assessment — Pimp Q&A
⚡
Why hold ACE-I/ARBs on the day of surgery?
›
Block RAAS → refractory hypotension on induction that doesn't respond to typical vasopressors. Especially dangerous with neuraxial anesthesia. Generally hold morning-of for elective cases.
⚡
GLP-1 agonist patient — how does this change your NPO assessment?
›
Semaglutide/GLP-1 agonists significantly delay gastric emptying. Even with adequate fasting, substantial gastric residual may remain. ASA guidance: hold weekly GLP-1 for 1 week. Consider RSI or point-of-care gastric ultrasound.
⚡
When is succinylcholine absolutely contraindicated?
›
K⁺ >5.5 mEq/L · Burns >48h post-injury · Crush injury/rhabdomyolysis · Denervation injuries (UMN/LMN) · Prolonged immobility · Personal/family history of MH · Pseudocholinesterase deficiency · Myopathies (Duchenne, etc.)
⚡
Mallampati III — what does it mean and what's your plan?
›
Only base of uvula visible = anticipated difficult direct laryngoscopy. Video laryngoscope as primary tool. Have SGA + fiberoptic ready. Second provider available. Discuss with attending. Consider awake FOI if also Class IV or multiple predictors.
⚡
What platelet count is the threshold for spinal anesthesia?
›
80,000–100,000/μL generally required. Below 50k = neuraxial contraindicated. Also consider trend (falling is more concerning than stable) and etiology of thrombocytopenia.
⚡
STOP-BANG score of 6 — how does this change your plan?
›
High risk OSA. Minimize opioids (multimodal: regional, NSAIDs, acetaminophen, dexamethasone). Anticipate difficult mask ventilation. CPAP in PACU. Semi-upright positioning. Consider overnight admission. Continue home CPAP through perioperative period.

Induction — Pimp Q&A
⚡
Why pre-oxygenate and what's your goal?
›
Replaces N₂ in FRC with O₂, creating an O₂ reservoir that extends safe apnea time. Goal: EtO₂ ≥85% (end-tidal O₂). Healthy adult: 8–10 min apnea safe. Obese/pregnant/pediatric: much shorter — 2–4 min. This is why we position and pre-ox before every induction.
⚡
Succinylcholine vs rocuronium for RSI — when do you choose which?
›
Succinylcholine: faster onset (45–60 sec), shorter duration (8–12 min) — ideal when airway concern exists and you want conditions to resolve quickly. Rocuronium 1.2mg/kg: comparable intubating conditions but longer duration (60–90 min) — use when succinylcholine contraindicated. Sugammadex 16mg/kg reverses roc in ~3 min if can't intubate can't oxygenate.
⚡
How do you confirm ETT placement?
›
Gold standard: continuous EtCO₂ waveform (≥6 breaths). Supporting: bilateral breath sounds on auscultation, chest rise, no epigastric gurgling, fogging of tube, SpO₂ maintained. CXR for depth if needed. EtCO₂ waveform alone is definitive — if no waveform, assume esophageal and re-intubate.
⚡
What is cricoid pressure and is it evidence-based?
›
Sellick's maneuver — posteriorly directed pressure on cricoid cartilage to compress esophagus and prevent passive regurgitation. Controversial: evidence is mixed and it can worsen laryngoscopic view. Current guidance: apply if trained, release if it obstructs view. Never delay intubation to apply it. Many centers have abandoned it.

Emergence — Pimp Q&A
⚡
What TOF ratio is required before extubation?
›
TOF ratio ≥0.9 (some say ≥1.0). Below 0.9 = residual neuromuscular blockade → upper airway obstruction, impaired respiratory effort, aspiration risk. Never extubate without confirming reversal quantitatively — subjective assessment (hand grip alone) is insufficient.
⚡
What is laryngospasm and how do you treat it?
›
Reflexive closure of vocal cords → partial or complete airway obstruction. Usually at emergence when patient is in "Stage II" (excitement phase). Treatment: 100% O₂, jaw thrust, CPAP, remove stimulus. If partial: continuous positive pressure usually breaks it. If complete (no air moving, SpO₂ dropping): Succinylcholine 0.1–0.5mg/kg IV (or 4mg/kg IM if no IV) → intubate if needed.
⚡
Neostigmine vs sugammadex — when do you use each?
›
Neostigmine: acetylcholinesterase inhibitor — works on ALL non-depolarizing NMBs. Must give with anticholinergic (glycopyrrolate) to block muscarinic effects (bradycardia, secretions). Ceiling effect — can't fully reverse deep block. Sugammadex: encapsulates rocuronium/vecuronium specifically. No ceiling. Can reverse deep block. No anticholinergic needed. Preferred when possible.

🔴 Can't Intubate Can't Oxygenate (CICO)
Cannot place airway AND cannot maintain oxygenation. This is a "can't wait" emergency — patient will die without immediate surgical airway.
Sequence — Every Second Counts
DECLARE CICO — call for help and difficult airway cart
Optimize mask ventilation (two-person, OPA/NPA)
Try supraglottic airway (LMA) — last attempt at non-surgical
If STILL can't oxygenate → SURGICAL AIRWAY
Do not delay further — hypoxic brain injury begins at 4–6 minutes
Needle cricothyrotomy (temporizing)
14G angiocath through cricothyroid membrane + jet ventilation
Surgical cricothyrotomy (definitive)
Scalpel-finger-bougie technique · Fastest and most reliable
Cricothyroid Membrane Location
Between thyroid cartilage (above) and cricoid cartilage (below). Palpate midline just below the Adam's apple. Horizontal incision, then vertical through membrane.

Succinylcholine mimics ACh → sustained depolarization → fasciculations → flaccid paralysis.
Non-depolarizing: competitive antagonism at NMJ → no fasciculations → reversed by anticholinesterases or sugammadex.
